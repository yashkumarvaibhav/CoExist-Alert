\begin{summarybox}[Architecture Read]
These diagrams show the four views reviewers need most: system components, detection-to-warning sequence, escalation and ownership workflow, and runtime deployment. The same architecture is implemented in the app and summarized in the Markdown companion document.
\end{summarybox}

\begin{decisionbox}{Reading note}
The diagram pages are intentionally landscape so the PDF remains as readable as the GitHub-rendered Markdown diagrams. Field sensors and demo signals are simulated in this proof of concept; alerting, audit logging, exports, live updates, and responder ownership are implemented in the app.
\end{decisionbox}

\begin{decisionbox}{False-positive path}
A weak single signal remains unconfirmed, is logged quietly, and expires without channel dispatch if no second source corroborates it inside the confirmation window.
\end{decisionbox}

\begin{decisionbox}{Ownership rule}
Unconfirmed signals promote only after high confidence or second-source corroboration; otherwise they expire without public alerting. The first acknowledging responder owns the incident. A paged senior can take over, but other responders cannot progress or resolve someone else's active incident.
\end{decisionbox}
\end{landscape}
